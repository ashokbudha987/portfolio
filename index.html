§1 Research Statement

My work sits at the intersection of natural language processing and applied machine learning: how systems can turn unstructured language — text, speech, broadcast audio — into reliable, verifiable knowledge. At LIS Nepal I took a natural-language-to-SQL system from proof of concept to a deployable product, building the parsing, intent-detection and embedding layers that made it work. Earlier, I built retrieval-augmented generation systems over structured knowledge bases together with evaluation pipelines using state-of-the-art generation metrics.

My undergraduate thesis research produced a peer-presented publication on a multi-modal pipeline for generating sports summaries from broadcast audio, metadata, and news text, reaching ROGUE scores comparable to state-of-the-art baselines. I am now looking to deepen this line of work in a doctoral program, with interests in:

  • Large language models & retrieval-augmented generation
  • Semantic parsing and natural-language interfaces to structured data
  • Multimodal learning (audio · text · metadata)
  • Speech processing and ASR systems for low-resource languages
  • Evaluation methodology for generative systems
